## **Effects of Electromagnetic Field of UHV Transmission** **Lines Exposure on Testis Tissue in Mice**

Duyan Geng [1], Guizhi Xu [1], Xianghong Zhang [2], Lingxiao Xing [2], Hongyong Guo [3], Weili Yan [1], and Youhua Wang [1]

Province-Ministry Joint Key Laboratory of Electromagnetic Field and Electrical Apparatus Reliability, Hebei University of

Technology, Tianjin 300130, China
Preclinical Medicine College of Hebei Medical University, Shijiazhuang 050017, China

Natural Science Foundation of Hebei Province, Shijiazhuang 050021, China

**The main goal of this study was to evaluate the possible effects of electromagnetic field (EMF) of transmission lines exposure on testis**
**tissue of mice. Forty adult BALB/c mice were divided into two groups. One group (twenty mice) has been done by daily exposures of 24**
**hours,** **and** **the other** **sham-exposed** **group** **(twenty** **mice)** **served as** **the** **control.** **After** **seven weeks** **of** **exposure,** **two groups** **of** **the mice**
**were sacrificed. The testis tissue was assessed by serum biochemical assay, histopathological examination and flow cytometric analysis**
**(FCM). EMF exposure did not significantly affect the distribution of DNA ploidy (1n, 2n and 4n cells), but significantly decreased the S**
**phase fraction and the Proliferation Index of testis germ cells.**

***Index*** ***Terms—*** **Effects,** **extremely** **low** **frequency** **(ELF)** **electromagnetic** **field** **(EMF)** **exposure,** **proliferation** **index,** **S** **phase** **fraction,**
**testis** **tissue.**


I. I NTRODUCTION
# A S THE ECONOMY in industrialized countries has main-tained rapid growth, the requirement of electric power is

increasing. The voltage and current of power transmission lines
have been improved to meet this development. Many large substations and ultra high voltage (UHV) power transmission lines
have been constructed in many countries.

However, in the late 1970s, Wertheimer and Leeper [1]
showed that the children living near electric wiring configurations have an increased risk of cancer. Since then, the possible
deleterious effects on human health due to exposure to the
electromagnetic field (EMF) of power transmission lines have
been of concern for several decades. In fact, the EMF was
classified as a ‘possibly carcinogenic to humans’ by International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) [2]. At present,
the biological effects of EMF and their possible consequences
upon human health began to raise more and more scientific
attention and became a recurrent subject of public debate. There
have been of considerable discussions about the response of
biological system to EMF exposure. Although, a large number
of long-term EMF exposed on animals had been done, the results were inconsistent. Data on the effects of electromagnetic
field on animals are inconsistent probably due to differences
in exposure levels, exposure durations, genotoxicity endpoints
and other experimental variables [3]–[6]. Therefore, animal
studies have concentrated on responses to electromagnetic field
at molecular level such as cell proliferation and other processes
required for cell growth and division.

The electromagnetic field induced by power transmission
lines belongs to an extremely low frequency (ELF) field and
can be characterized by many different physical parameters. In

The purpose of the study is to evaluate whether the electromagnetic field at ground level beneath UHV power lines could

to model the electromagnetic environment (elliptical polarization field) beneath alternating current (AC) 1000 kV line carrying 3 kA–4 kA at a frequency of 50 Hertz (50 Hz). The condition that mice are exposed to is similar to that of human bodies.

II. M ATERIALS AND M ETHODS

*A.* *Animals*

Four week-old adult BALB/c mice weighing 18–22 g were
purchased from the laboratory animal center of Hebei Medical
University. The mice were housed under a 12 h light/12 h dark
cycle, constant temperature (18–22 C) and 30%–70% humidity
and were fed mineral water. Forty mice (20 males and 20 females) were divided at random into two groups (10 males and
10 females in each group). Five mice were housed in one cage.
One of the two groups was continuously exposed to the exposure
system for 24 hours/day for seven weeks and the other exposed
to sham conditions served as the controls.

*B.* *Exposure System*

The system mainly contained a step-down transformer, a
step-up transformer, an insulating board, three-phase voltage
lines, three-phase current lines, and a group of loads. An electric
field was induced by three-phase (50 Hz) voltage lines which

*C.* *Assessment Method*

*1)* *Serum Biochemical Assay:* Mice were sacrificed by cervical dislocation under light ether anesthesia. Blood samples
were collected at second day and centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 10
min. Twenty serum samples were taken from each experimental
and control animals (ten samples each group of animals). Blood
samples were stored at C until biochemical analyses.

The testes tissue was homogenized in Teflon-glass homogenizer with a buffer containing 1–5% potassium chloride to obtain 1:10 (w/v) whole homogenate. The values of -GGT activity were expressed as U/L for plasma.

*2)* *Histopathological Examination:* The testes were removed
for histopathological examination. Formalin-fixed testis was
embedded in paraffin, sectioned at 4–6 m and stained with
hematoxylin and eosin (H-E) for evaluation by transmission
electron microscopy (TEM). Histological analysis of testis
tissue including Johnsen’s testicular biopsy score [9] was
performed for control and exposed groups. All tubules were
evaluated systematically, and three tubules were evaluated for
each animal.

*3)* *Preparation* *of* *Germ* *Cell* *Rich* *Fraction:* The mice
were killed by cervical dislocation and the testes excised and
placed in precooled phosphate buffered saline (PBS). After
rinsing three times in PBS, the fleshly isolated segments were
additionally fixed for 30 min in 70% ethanol and air dried.
The tunica was removed and segments (about 2 mm in length)
of seminiferous tubules were isolated. After centrifugation at
800 rmp for 2 min, the supernatant was removed. The germ cell
rich fractions were stained with ethidium bromide (50 mg/L).

III. R ESULTS

*1)* *Serum* *Biochemical* *Data:* Biochemical assay results
showed that no significant differences in serum levels of

-GGT were indicated between the experimental group and the
control group .
*2)* *Testicular Biopsy Score:* Significant pathological changes
were not observed in the testis of two groups of mice under
TEM. It means that there were no differences between the ex
perimental group and the control group.

*3)* *Flow* *Cytometry* *Analysis:* There was a decrease in the
percentage of the haploid-cells in the exposed groups than in
the controls, and increase in the tetraploid-cells, as shown in
Fig. 4. The percentage of the diploid-cells in the exposed groups
was almost equal to that of control groups. Consequently, these
changes were not statistically significant.

There was an increase in the percentage of G1 phase fraction
of testis tissue in the exposed groups than in the controls, and
also G2-phase had the same development. But the changes were
not statistically significant, too. The percent of the cell population in the S phase in experimental groups was significantly
lower than in the control groups, as shown in Fig. 5.

Cell proliferation was determined by counting the resulting
cell numbers with a hemocytometer, using 0.4% trypan blue
solution. The Proliferation Index of testis germ cells in experimental groups was significantly lower than that in control
groups, as shown in Fig. 6.

IV. C ONCLUSION AND D ISCUSSIONS

It is now clear that ELF EMF can produce statistically significant biological responses [10]. Especially, the exposure of male
rats to electromagnetic field leads to reduction in their fertility

[10]. Relation between ELF EMF and changes of cell cycle and
in vivo and in vitro studies on cell proliferation are the main
subjects of the studies, in which combined power (50 Hz or
60 Hz) frequency electric and magnetic fields effects are investigated [11], [12], [14]. In light of the results of these studies,
we presented a novel exposure system to study the potential
health effects of ELF EMF on an in vivo system, BABL/c mice.
The natural potential risk factors, such as temperature, humidity
and other deleterious gases, can be eliminated in this exposure
condition.

In previous studies, Syariffuddin Mahmudsyah and Sudarti

[11] reported that extremely low frequency electromagnetic
fields exposure emitted by 500 kV high-voltage transmission
lines have decreased the numbers of spermatogonium, primer
spermatocyte and spermatid of white rats. In their study, the
intensities of electric and magnetic fields were respectively no
more than 3 kV/m and 2 T. And the animal exposure was
continuously held on the average of 14 h/d in the cage and
10 h/d around the cage during 4 and 8 weeks. MUHTEREM

AYDIN *et* *al.* [12] investigated the effects of electromagnetic
fields generated by 170 kV (50 Hz) high power lines on the
epididymal sperm characteristics, biochemical parameters
testosterone levels and histopathology of testis. The intensities
of electric and magnetic fields were respectively no more than
2 kV and 5 T. The study reported that adverse effects on cell
proliferation, decrease in testestorone level and germ cell population were determined on rats. But no effect on sperm motility
and abnormal sperm level were found. Jin Sang Lee *et al.* [13]
evaluated the effects of 60 Hz ELF EMF for eight weeks of
exposure on germ cell apoptosis in the testis of mice. The result
indicated that continuous exposure to ELF EMF may induce
testicular germ cell apoptosis in mice. The similar results had
been reported by investigation of guinea pigs exposed to EMF

[14]. The results presented here in these studies indicated that
electromagnetic field exposure significantly affected the testes
and may cause histological changes.

This study indicates that long-term exposure to controllable
ELF EMF environment produced by actual power transmission
lines has decreased the S phase fraction and the Proliferation
Index of testis germ cells in BALB/c mice. Consequently, DNA
Synthesis would possibly be inhibited. This phenomenon has
been observed in above mentioned independent experiments.
The phenomenon is not induced by thermal effect. In conclusion, although DNA ploidy (1n, 2n and 4n cells) investigation
revealed that there were no clear carcinogenic effects or differences in DNA content, and significant pathological changes
were not observed in the testis, this finding reinforces the observation from other experiments that power frequency electromagnetic field exposure does stimulate the percent of the cell
population in the S phase and cell proliferation rate of testis
germ cells in BALB/c mice. The conclusion indicates that ELF
EMF is possible mutagens or co-mutagens. It is well known
that the World Health Organization (WHO) defines health as
the state of complete physical, mental and social well being and
not merely the absence of disease or infirmity [15]. Despite the
effects of electromagnetic field on the bodies of humans and
animals are significantly different, the adverse effects on the
spermatogenesis of BABL/c mice can not be tolerated by the
majority of people. For this reason, further investigations are
needed to identify if lower intensity of ELF EMF of power transmission lines is the mutagens or the co-mutagens.
